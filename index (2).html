I am a Ph.D. student in Plant, Environmental, and Soil Sciences at Louisiana State University and a Graduate Research Assistant in the LSU AgCenter Forage Quality Laboratory, advised by Dr. Kun-Jun Han.

My research focuses on making forage quality assessment faster and more scalable. I combine laboratory wet chemistry, near-infrared spectroscopy, UAV multispectral imaging, and machine learning to predict the nutritive value of forages across working farms in Louisiana.

Research Interests

Forage Quality Analysis

Wet chemistry for NDF, ADF, ADL, crude protein, ether extract, ash, and IVTDMD as the reference data behind every prediction model.

NIRS Calibration

Near-infrared spectroscopy calibration models that predict forage nutritive value quickly, without full laboratory analysis.

UAV Remote Sensing

Multispectral drone imagery to estimate forage energy and total digestible nutrients (TDN) at the field scale.

Machine Learning for Forage Prediction

Regression models linking spectral data to forage quality, and testing how well they transfer between farms and seasons.

Pasture Soil Health

Connecting soil, drone, and biomass data to understand what drives pasture productivity.

Alternative Forage Systems

Evaluating small grains as alternatives to annual ryegrass in Louisiana forage production.
